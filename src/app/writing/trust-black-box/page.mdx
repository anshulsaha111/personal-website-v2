# Building Trust in Black-Box Systems

Users don't need to understand how AI works. They need to understand *when to rely on it*.

That distinction is everything.

## The Explainability Trap

For years, the AI industry has been obsessed with explainability. Show users why the model made a decision. Add confidence scores. Provide feature attributions.

Most users don't care.

What they care about is: *Can I trust this?*

## Trust Signals That Work

After shipping AI features to millions of users, I've noticed what actually builds trust:

**Acknowledge uncertainty.** "I'm not sure" is more trustworthy than false confidence.

**Degrade gracefully.** When the AI isn't sure, hand off to a human or a simpler system.

**Be consistent.** Erratic behavior destroys trust faster than errors.

**Show your work selectively.** Not everything needs explanation. But high-stakes decisions do.

## The Trust Ladder

Trust isn't binary. It's built incrementally:

1. Try it once with low stakes
2. See it work correctly
3. Catch it before it fails
4. Recover well from failure
5. Start relying on it

The best AI products are designed to climb this ladder with users.
