# Why Most AI Products Fail at Launch

There's a pattern I've seen across dozens of AI launches. The demo is magical. The pilot goes well. Then you ship to production and everything falls apart.

## The Demo Problem

Demos are optimized for the happy path. They show what the model *can* do, not what it *will* do when faced with the chaos of real user input.

Every AI product team eventually learns this lesson. The question is whether they learn it before or after launch.

## What Actually Goes Wrong

The failure modes are predictable:

**Data drift.** The training data looks nothing like production data. Users type things you never imagined. Edge cases aren't edge cases—they're 30% of your traffic.

**Latency expectations.** That 2-second response time felt fine in demos. In production, users click away after 500ms.

**Trust calibration.** Users either trust the AI too much or not at all. Neither is good.

## Building for Survival

The products that survive contact with real users share a few traits:

1. They ship with monitoring from day one
2. They have graceful degradation built in
3. They set expectations correctly upfront

None of this is glamorous. But it's the difference between a demo and a product.
